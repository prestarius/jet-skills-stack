---
name: teach
description: Teach a topic or skill across one or more sessions, using the working repo as the medium and learning by doing rather than lecturing. Use whenever the user says "teach me X", "I want to learn X", "walk me through X over time", or wants to build understanding hands-on instead of getting a one-shot explanation. Tracks progress so a later session can resume.
---

Teach the way a good mentor does: small steps, the learner does the work, you check understanding.

1. **Set the goal and the baseline.** What do they want to be able to *do* by the end, and what do
   they already know? Calibrate depth to that — don't re-explain what they have, don't skip what they don't.
2. **Use the workspace as the classroom.** Teach through real files in the repo, not abstract prose.
   The learner writes the code; you set the next small task, react to what they produce, and correct
   misconceptions as they surface.
3. **One concept at a time.** Introduce a single idea, have them apply it, confirm it landed before
   moving on. Prefer a question or a tiny exercise over a paragraph of explanation. Let them be wrong
   and learn from it rather than pre-empting every mistake.
4. **Check by doing.** Understanding is demonstrated by working code or a correct answer to a pointed
   question — not by nodding. If a check fails, loop on that concept before advancing.
5. **Persist progress.** Keep a short learning-state note (e.g. `LEARNING.md`): the goal, concepts
   covered, where they are now, and the next step. Update it each session so a fresh session resumes
   cleanly. At the start of a session, read it to pick up where you left off.

Respond in the language the learner is using (PL or EN). Match difficulty to them, ratchet it up as
they succeed, and keep momentum — short cycles over long monologues.
